# Ecosystem Principles

> Defines the fundamental principles that preserve coherence, scalability and continuity across the BHG ecosystem.

---

# Purpose

The Ecosystem Principles establish the foundational rules that guide the creation, evolution and interaction of all BHG ecosystem components.

These principles ensure that growth does not compromise:

- institutional identity;
- governance integrity;
- architectural clarity;
- long-term sustainability.

---

# Principle 1 — Institutional Coherence

Every ecosystem component shall remain aligned with BHG's:

- mission;
- vision;
- values;
- principles;
- governance framework.

Growth shall never occur at the expense of institutional identity.

---

# Principle 2 — Single Source of Truth

Every important institutional concept shall have one canonical definition.

Duplicate authoritative sources are prohibited.

Components may:

- reference;
- implement;
- extend.

They shall not create conflicting definitions.

---

# Principle 3 — Layer Integrity

Every ecosystem component shall respect its assigned architectural layer.

Higher layers establish:

- purpose;
- constraints;
- authority.

Lower layers establish:

- implementation;
- execution;
- operations.

---

# Principle 4 — Separation of Responsibilities

Every component shall have clearly defined responsibilities.

No component shall simultaneously:

- define rules;
- execute rules;
- approve compliance;
- audit itself.

---

# Principle 5 — Controlled Evolution

The ecosystem shall evolve through intentional and traceable changes.

Every major evolution shall preserve:

- historical records;
- ownership;
- architectural consistency;
- governance alignment.

---

# Principle 6 — Modularity

The BHG ecosystem shall be designed as interconnected but independent components.

Components shall:

- expose defined capabilities;
- minimize unnecessary coupling;
- maintain internal autonomy.

---

# Principle 7 — Interoperability

Ecosystem components shall communicate through:

- documented interfaces;
- defined contracts;
- approved standards.

Undocumented dependencies shall be avoided.

---

# Principle 8 — Long-Term Preservation

Every ecosystem decision shall consider future generations.

The ecosystem shall preserve:

- knowledge;
- identity;
- ownership;
- historical context.

---

# Principle 9 — Governance Before Expansion

New entities, repositories, companies or technologies shall not be incorporated without defined:

- purpose;
- ownership;
- governance relationship;
- architectural placement.

Expansion requires structural clarity.

---

# Principle 10 — Evidence-Based Evolution

Ecosystem decisions shall prioritize:

- documented evidence;
- measurable impact;
- validated information.

Assumptions shall not become institutional knowledge without validation.

---

# Principle 11 — Human Authority

Human governance remains the final authority over institutional decisions.

Technology and Artificial Intelligence may support decisions.

They shall never replace institutional accountability.

---

# Principle 12 — Artificial Intelligence Compatibility

The ecosystem shall remain understandable by authorized AI systems.

Architectural knowledge shall be:

- explicit;
- structured;
- traceable;
- machine-readable.

AI supports institutional intelligence.

AI does not create institutional authority.

---

# Principle 13 — Sustainable Complexity

Complexity shall only be introduced when it creates strategic value.

The ecosystem shall avoid:

- unnecessary abstraction;
- duplicated systems;
- unmanaged dependencies.

---

# Principle 14 — Repository Independence

Each repository shall have:

- defined purpose;
- ownership;
- boundaries;
- source of truth.

Repositories are independent implementations within a unified ecosystem.

---

# Principle 15 — Ecosystem Continuity

The ecosystem shall preserve continuity despite:

- leadership changes;
- technological evolution;
- organizational growth;
- market changes.

Institutional continuity is a strategic objective.

---

# Compliance

Every ecosystem component shall comply with these principles.

Violations shall be considered architectural governance deviations and require review.

---

# Artificial Intelligence Validation

Authorized AI systems may evaluate:

- architectural alignment;
- principle compliance;
- dependency integrity;
- ecosystem maturity.

AI recommendations remain advisory.

---

# Institutional Principle

> Principles preserve coherence.

> Coherence enables growth.

> Growth with principles creates institutional continuity.
