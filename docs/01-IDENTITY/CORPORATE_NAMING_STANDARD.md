# Corporate Naming Standard

> Defines the official naming framework of the Breto's Holding Group ecosystem.

---

# Purpose

The Corporate Naming Standard establishes the rules used to create, evaluate and maintain official names across the BHG ecosystem.

Names represent institutional identity.

A correct naming system enables:

- recognition;
- scalability;
- governance clarity;
- knowledge preservation;
- technological interoperability.

---

# Naming Principles

All BHG names shall follow these principles.

---

# 1. Meaning Before Appearance

Names shall communicate purpose, identity or strategic intent.

A name shall not exist only because it sounds attractive.

---

# 2. Long-Term Validity

Names shall remain relevant beyond:

- temporary projects;
- technologies;
- market conditions;
- individual contributors.

---

# 3. Institutional Consistency

Names shall align with:

- BHG identity;
- corporate language;
- brand architecture;
- ecosystem structure.

---

# 4. Uniqueness

Every official name shall be distinguishable.

Duplicate or confusing names are prohibited.

---

# Naming Categories

BHG names are classified into the following categories.

---

# Corporate Names

Identify legal or organizational entities.

Examples:

- Holding companies;
- subsidiaries;
- business units.

Corporate names require governance approval.

---

# Brand Names

Identify market-facing identities.

They represent:

- products;
- services;
- experiences;
- customer relationships.

---

# Product Names

Identify specific offerings.

Examples:

- software platforms;
- financial products;
- digital services.

---

# System Names

Identify internal technological or operational systems.

Examples:

- governance systems;
- automation engines;
- infrastructure platforms.

---

# Repository Names

Identify controlled digital repositories.

Repository names shall:

- be descriptive;
- remain stable;
- represent repository purpose.

---

# Asset Names

Identify institutional assets.

Examples:

- intellectual property;
- frameworks;
- methodologies;
- internal models.

---

# Naming Rules

Official names shall:

- use approved terminology;
- avoid ambiguity;
- avoid unnecessary abbreviations;
- avoid temporary references;
- preserve institutional meaning.

---

# Abbreviation Policy

Abbreviations may be used when:

- they improve clarity;
- they are officially defined;
- they are registered in the institutional glossary.

Unknown abbreviations shall not become official terminology.

---

# Naming Approval Process

New official names shall follow:

1. Name proposal.
2. Identity evaluation.
3. Conflict verification.
4. Brand compatibility review.
5. Governance approval.
6. Registration.

---

# Naming Registry

All official names shall be registered within the appropriate institutional registry.

The registry shall maintain:

- name;
- category;
- owner;
- creation date;
- status;
- relationships;
- historical changes.

---

# Artificial Intelligence Compatibility

AI systems may use naming standards to:

- identify entities;
- classify assets;
- detect conflicts;
- maintain semantic consistency.

AI systems shall not create official names without governance approval.

---

# Naming Conflicts

Potential conflicts include:

- duplicate names;
- misleading names;
- unauthorized usage;
- inconsistent terminology.

Conflicts shall be evaluated through governance processes.

---

# Naming Evolution

Names may evolve when:

- strategic direction changes;
- legal requirements require modification;
- brand restructuring occurs.

Changes shall preserve historical records.

Previous names shall remain traceable.

---

# Compliance

Any official BHG entity, product, system or repository shall comply with this standard.

Unauthorized naming practices represent institutional inconsistency.

---

# Institutional Principle

> Names preserve identity.

> Identity preserves institutional continuity.
